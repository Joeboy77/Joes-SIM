The Little Man does the same three things over and over, for every instruction. This is the
**instruction cycle**, and real processors do it billions of times a second.

1. **Fetch.** Look at the <Highlight component="PC">program counter</Highlight> to find the
   next mailbox. Copy its number to the <Highlight component="MAR">address register</Highlight>,
   read that mailbox into the <Highlight component="MDR">data register</Highlight>, and copy it
   onto the <Highlight component="IR">instruction register</Highlight>. Add one to the program
   counter, ready for next time.
2. **Decode.** The <Highlight component="CONTROL">control unit</Highlight> splits the
   instruction into what to do and which mailbox.
3. **Execute.** Do it: load, add, store, branch, input or output.

Each of these small moves is a **micro-step**. Press **Micro** in the simulator below to go one
micro-step at a time, and read the explanation underneath after each press. Press the back
button to go backwards.

## Why the program counter moves first

The program counter goes up by one during the fetch, before the instruction runs. That is why
a branch works: if the instruction is a branch, it simply overwrites the program counter with a
new address. Next time round, the fetch starts from there.
